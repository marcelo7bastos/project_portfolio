02 / WHO’S IN THE LAB

A way to keep inventing.

As a child, I dreamed of being an inventor. I became an economist. Data science and artificial intelligence brought me back to what I wanted to do then: have an idea, build something, and see if it works.

I can now put prototypes online that once would have taken weeks, months, or even years. Sometimes testing shows that an idea is worth pursuing. Sometimes it was just for fun. And occasionally a simple solution can address the most urgent part of a complex problem when time is short.

AI speeds up building and helps us explore solutions. The judgment is still ours. We ask the question, examine what was produced, and decide whether it serves the problem at hand. I believe that ability to think critically will distinguish the professionals of the future.

In public service

At Brazil’s Ministry of Agrarian Development and Family Farming, I work with rural credit data and family farming registries to support analysis and policy monitoring.

  • Data engineering. I automate extraction from large sources, validate file structures, and document runs so the outputs can be checked.
  • Registry integration. I document linkage criteria, reference periods, and the distinction between individuals, households, and attributes.
  • Indicators. I organize workflows that can be resumed, recording sources, reference periods, and delivery dates.

Finding the personal identifier is only the beginning

Suppose Ana appears in two registries. One lists her in household A in May. The other lists her in household B in June, with the income field left blank. This is a fictional example.

Individual matched
Brazil’s individual taxpayer number (CPF) allows us to identify Ana in both registries.
Household linked
The linkage points to different households in different months. Check the reference period and unit of analysis before comparing them.
Attribute available
A blank field means the information is missing. Treating it as zero income would change the result.

I record the person match, household linkage, and attribute availability separately, alongside each value’s source and reference month. This keeps the match rate distinct from the coverage of the variable under study.
